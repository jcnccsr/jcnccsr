What is real ? What is reality ? How do we know if what we perceived as reality is but a dream ? How do we know we're not in a simulation ? How do we even know what we know ?
{:.lead}

* toc
{:toc .large-only}

These musings could sound like a crazy talk. These could probably give somebody an impression of an interesting conspiracy or a doze of skepticism. That's fair. It seems obvious to anyone with common sense, right ? The world is what we see and feel, smell and hear. That is reality.

But how do we know that's true ? I was once told that a claim is true if it [corresponds to reality][correspondence]. The problem is, it doesn't give me new information. And so it just loops back in a circle, leaving me asking the same question, "What is reality ?" 

So what is it ? People would say that it's the universe that exists independent of our minds. This doesn't refute the claims though of us being just a brain in a vat, or in a lucid dream, or in a simulation. The vat is still objective, or the computer or whatever that is supplying me the signals that my brain translate as sensations, pieces which then my brain uses to creatively contruct a subjective world which I then call 'reality'.

The question needs to change. To make this question to work, we need to somehow map our perceived things to what actually exists objectively. How do I know that what I perceive as reality is the objective reality, the world that is independent of my mind. Everything I perceive is through my senses. All my senses are, to simply put it, organs that convert sense-data into electrical impulses that go to my brain, and then my brain process and interpret it, which make the whole perception thing, subjective. And now, I'm back at square one: how do I know that the things perceived by my senses were not just electrical impulses made by the vat's simulated world, and my brain is just interpreting it as the world ? How about me and everything else being in a grand SIMS? How do we falsify the claim that I'm just a simulated character in this simulated world running on some kind of computer of some pan-dimensional beings ?

What comes to mind is a trap, and I'm inside of it. Am I the brain, or the brain is the trap that I can't get out of ? My computer, my keyboard, all these things I think I know were seen, touched, felt, and smelled by my senses, which were then turned into some kind of electrical signals that were then interpreted by my brain as "clickity" and "thock", and that pleasurable tactile sensation when my finger pressed the keys. I did felt those. That clickity noises, that thock sound a key makes when abruptly pressed, I know I heard those. I may be a simulation. I may be a disembodied mind, but I know that I experienced those sensations. But to determine if my computer and my keyboard actually exists outside of my mind, is a different category.

That's the key. The experiences I have, is true, unless I'm lying to myself, of course. I know I see the screen of one of my monitors, even though there's always a possibility that there's no monitor at all, and all these things in front of me are mere illusions: albeit a deep one. There's no way for me to go out of my brain so I can bypass this sort of biological message relay, and instead directly observe what's out there.

Out-of-body experience. Astral projection. That's what it's called, or at least ones of its names. It's an interesting thing. If this is true, this could actually let me observe the reality itself, not the one created by my brain from electrical lego pieces. But how does that actually work ? I'm an disembodied mind, I imagine, without a brain. I would say that whatever "me" is, is what my brain have. When a person hit his head hard enough, there's almost always a consequence of change in that person. It's so interesting of a plot: a person getting into an accident then either losing its memories or mind. It's probably why creative people write about it, and turn it into stories in books and even in movies. But why is that ? If I can survive in an out-of-body experience, how do I keep all my mental faculties intact ? And what method do an out-of-body me use to even "see" the world (whatever that is) when I'm out of my body which has my eyes. I won't be able to use my eyes even if I can somehow bring these with me, because I need my brain to be able to have a use of these too. And it's the trap of a brain that I wanted to escape from in the first place. An idea of being a disembodied mind surely is an interesting one. But it doesn't makes sense to me.

I heard some stories about out-of-body experience, claiming they've seen their body lying down in third-person perspective, listened to the doctors talking, probably about their internal organs (not sure about that. I've never been in an emergency room, hopefully it stays that way), probing and poking those entrails here and there with their weird tools. But how do these people who allegedly died or came out of their body "see", or "hear", or even "think" though is beyond me. Comatose people rarely (or never, probably) came back to consciousness and speak about knowing things they came to know while being out of their body.cNow, I have to reconcile my understanding of "being conscious" and "being comatose".

The thing that makes this claim unconvincing enough though is the experiment done by researchers (I can't cite the source on top of my head) in emergency rooms. So what they did is they placed a digital display on top of the cupboard near the patients' bed. If even just one of the patients who are dying experienced being out of their body, looks down and see their own body lying on the bed, would also more likely to find the digital display with some randomized integers displayed on it. It would really be very interesting if someone comes forward and say, "You know, when I was on the bed at the hospital, I experienced the out-of-body phenomenon and saw the doctor hand-waving the cotton swab he dropped inside my tummy and I also saw this weird LCD with numbers 424242 on it". That would be a really interesting testimony. And at this point I know that something needs to be done with that cotton swab too.

All of these claims about unfalsifiable worlds, be it a brain signals of simulated worlds or Descartes' Evil Demon's trickeries are irrelevant though. I could be a simulated being, a cosmic Sims character, and it won't really matter. The experiences I have is true. And I have the capability of replicating these experiences if I want to, with consistent results. If I lift my keyboard up the desk and let it go, I expect it to fall down. I do this four times, ten times, the result will be the same. It's then reasonable for me to conclude that if I do this action again, there's a really good chance that my keyboard will fall down, the same way it did in my last ten experiments. This is very consistent, and other people have done this too, with the same result, that they calculated the acceleration of things falling down to be $$9.8m/s^2$$.

Now, there's probably no way for anyone to absolutely ascertain that the keyboard will still fall downward if I (or anyone) repeat the experiment the nth time with the same condition, but it is reasonable to say that my belief is true, that whenever I lift my keyboard off the desk and let it go, the keyboard will fall straight downward. Interestingly, Charles Sanders Peirce use this relationship between belief, action, and consequence to formulate a way to functionally label claims that involves objects outside of our mind as truth, false, or irrelevant. When we act on something, that action produces a consequence. It's a very elegant way of determining the "true" and "false" values of my beliefs, no matter if I'm inside a simulation game, or in a cosmic child's dream, or in a vat.
